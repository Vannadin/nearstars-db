# Mixture rule — checklist

The solver stacks one pure material per layer. A layer holding two materials at once has no
representation, and that single hole surfaces as two unrelated-looking symptoms.

- **Saturn is 20.7 % too large.** The `--table` output shows the same derived radius for
  Jupiter and Saturn, because n = 1 gives R = √(πK/2G) with K a single constant. There is
  no residual to reduce: it is one output held against two planets. What Saturn needs is
  the heavy elements dissolved in its envelope, and K has no axis for them.
- **Undifferentiated bodies are declined.** Metal that never segregated sits mixed through
  the silicate, and the decline message already names what it would take: "혼합물
  상태방정식(부피 가법 혼합 또는 Voigt-Reuss-Hill 평균)".

Both are the same defect. One mixture rule closes two rows of the domain table.

## Why now

The giant branch landed with two anchors, one passing and one failing, and the failing one
was the reason the branch is graded `analog` below Jupiter. Until the envelope can carry
metals, that grade cannot improve and the Saturn residual cannot move.

## Scope

- `eos.py` — a `Mixture` that composes materials by the additive volume law, with the
  constants, their sources and their validity limits in the comment beside them.
- `interior.py` — `envelope_z` as a declared input; the undifferentiated path solves
  instead of declining; the giant grading rule re-judged against the new anchor count.
- `test_giant.py` / `test_interior.py` — anchors and generated tables.
- Documentation: relation, constants, domain rows, sources. Nothing else.
- No board edits. A b's C/MR² is reported, not applied.

## Out of scope

- The mass axis. Adding Z to K leaves R = √(πK/2G) with no M in it, so two giants of the
  same Z still get the same radius. That axis is piecewise polytropes and it is separate.
- Diluted (fuzzy) cores. This is one homogeneous Z in the envelope. Whatever residual is
  left after that belongs to the distribution, and gets named rather than fitted.
- Partial differentiation. Fully mixed or fully layered; the in-between is a third thing.
- Lighter rock properties. That waits on the rocky roster showing a decline, and it shows
  none today.
- Ice giants and sub-Neptunes, each separate.
