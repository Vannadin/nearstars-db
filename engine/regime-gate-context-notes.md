**Three holes, each named, each fatal on its own for the locked branch:**
1. **ν has no value anywhere in RM22.** Appendix A.2's η_c is *"the dynamical viscosity of a mineral"* in eq. 36's
   Ra_c — a different equation and a different layer, explicitly a different substance. The exponent is weak
   (ν^(−1/3); the authors: *"depends mainly on the Rayleigh number, Ω, and D, and weakly on the core
   thermodynamic properties"*) but it is one unknown input, declared as such.
2. **q_conv is not defined.** One phrase — *"the convective heat flux generated by the core"* — and no equation;
   the appendix ends at Q_c (W) and the text starts at q_conv with no sentence joining them. Dimensionally
   W/m² (parallel seat's derivation, not printed). Total or super-adiabatic excess is undecided, and both
   readings have textual support. **Our Earth's 2.75 vs 4.91 TW (Brief 60 / C14) hangs exactly here.**
3. **The printed equation does not reproduce the paper's own Table 8** (parallel seat's scaling test on the five
   Solar-System bodies, normalised so ν and λ cancel): Ro_ℓ ∝ Ra_Q^(2/5) alone gives Mercury 0.94 · Venus 1.24 ·
   Mars 0.88 · Ganymede 0.75 of the printed values; the full printed form (with Ω^(1/3) D^(2/3)) gives 0.26 ·
   0.20 · 0.96 · 1.02 — **4–5× off on the two slow rotators**, and the least-squares exponent (P^1.156) matches
   Ra_Q^(2/5) alone (P^1.200), not the full form (P^0.867). The cause is the Ω factor, not D. Whichever form is
   chosen disagrees with the paper somewhere. **And Table 8 is not a validation**: *"we have found that the value
   for the core thermal conductivity better fitting the observed magnetic moment and local Rossby number is
   60 W/m/K"* — k was fitted to that table.

**Three of RM22's own defects go to the index** (`paper-defects.md`): the Ro_ℓ equation vs its Table 8 (4–5×);
λ_m = 1.32 m²/s against its own eq. 24, 1/(μ₀σ) with σ = 1.36×10⁶ S/m → **0.585**, a 2.25× disagreement with
both values sourced to (D); 0.12 called a *"local Rossby number"* in one place and a *"Rayleigh number"* in

objects of the solar system with this characteristic. In this case we use equation 20 for estimating ℳ."* And
